My desk looks almost identical to how it looked a year ago. Same monitor, same chair, same mess of cables I keep meaning to tidy. What's different is what actually happens there, and increasingly, how little of it requires me to be sitting in front of it at all.

## The setup

Three things do most of the work now:

1. **A ticket system** for describing what I want built, and why.
2. **An agent** that picks up labeled tickets and does the implementation.
3. **A phone**, for the parts of the loop that don't need a keyboard.

None of that is exotic. What changed is the order of operations. The desk used to be where the work happened. Now it's mostly where the work gets *reviewed* &mdash; the actual typing happens somewhere else, on a schedule that doesn't care whether I'm at my desk or not.

## What stayed the same

I still think through problems the same way I always have: write down what's actually wrong, argue with myself about the options, pick one, and see it through. The tools around that process changed. The process didn't.

The desk that "runs itself" is a bit of an exaggeration &mdash; it still needs someone deciding what's worth building and reading the result carefully. But the part where I had to be physically present for the typing to happen, that part is mostly gone, and I don't miss it.
